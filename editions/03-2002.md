# Edition 3 — 2002

M. Morris Mano, *Digital Design*, 3rd ed., Prentice Hall, ISBN 9780130621214. Catalog entries for this edition use 2001 or 2002. [Edition and back-cover records](SOURCES.md#e3)

## Changes from the second edition

| Topic | Comparison |
| --- | --- |
| Combinational design | The second edition taught analysis, design, arithmetic and code conversion in Ch. 4, then continued with combinational components in §§5-1–5-6. The third brings these together in Ch. 4, including arithmetic, comparators, decoders, encoders and multiplexers. |
| Memory and programmable logic | The second edition split ROM, PLA and PAL (§§5-7–5-9) from RAM, decoding and error correction (§§7-7–7-9). The third brings those subjects together in Ch. 7. |
| Minimization | The second edition's separate tabulation sequence (§§3-9–3-12, pp. 102–109) has no counterpart at the end of the third edition's Ch. 3. Its §3-9 introduces Verilog before the chapter exercises and references. The body and exercises still cover five-variable maps. |
| Flip-flop design | The second-edition preface already describes greater use of D flip-flops in its design examples. The third continues that emphasis and still teaches JK and T devices in Ch. 5. |
| Register-transfer design | Ch. 8 develops the RTL expression of register operations, HDL modeling and datapath/controller design from the earlier ASM material. The third edition includes a sequential multiplier model and testbench. |

## Verilog reading route

The back cover reports nine distributed Verilog readings. The route below follows the section identifiers checked against the English body and the contents and body of Pearson's authorized Spanish translation, which fills gaps in the local English extract. Section 11-19 adds lab practice after the instructional readings. [Back-cover revision summary](SOURCES.md#e3) · [English body](SOURCES.md#e3-body) · [Spanish contents, preface and body](SOURCES.md#e3-spanish)

| Design step | Book reading | Companion practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start with a Boolean function | §3-9, structural models, Boolean assignments and truth-table primitives | [Minimization example](../examples/README.md#minimization) |
| Simulate combinational blocks | §4-11, gate-level, dataflow and behavioral models | [Combinational examples](../examples/README.md#combinational) |
| Describe clocked state | §5-5, assignments, flip-flops, latches and sequential models | [Sequence detector](../examples/README.md#sequential) |
| Specify register and counter operations | §6-6, structural and behavioral models | [Registers and counters](../examples/README.md#registers--counters) |
| Describe a memory array | §7-2, memory-model subsection and HDL Example 7-1 | [Synchronous RAM](../examples/README.md#memory--error-detection) |
| Express register transfers | §8-2, RTL in HDL | [Register-transfer design](../notes/08-rtl.md) |
| Model a controller and datapath | §8-5, structural and RTL HDL descriptions of the design example | [Datapath and controller](../examples/README.md#datapath--controller) |

Older Verilog examples use procedural `always` blocks and `reg` declarations. Identify the hardware from the activation conditions and assignments. Then use the [companion HDL route](../examples/README.md#hdl-reading-route) to express the same behavior with modern SystemVerilog or VHDL. Check the observed result after the simulator has applied deferred signal updates.

## Historical chapters and labs

Ch. 9 still teaches asynchronous sequential logic and Ch. 10 still analyzes digital IC families. Ch. 11 retains physical experiments and adds the §11-19 Verilog simulation supplements. Ch. 12 covers standard graphic symbols. The fourth edition retains these standalone subjects. [Third-edition body](SOURCES.md#e3-body) · [Fourth-edition contents](SOURCES.md#e4)
