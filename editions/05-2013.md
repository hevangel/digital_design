## Changes from the fourth edition

The preface describes a foundation course centered on Boolean logic, synchronous state machines, datapath/controller design and mainstream CMOS. The revision omits the fourth edition's standalone chapters on asynchronous sequential logic and digital ICs. The authors report removing dedicated asynchronous machine coverage and specialized circuit-level topics such as bipolar transistors, resistor–transistor logic, diode–transistor logic and emitter-coupled logic. They also report streamlining Karnaugh-map instruction, adding end-of-chapter web search topics, and using devices from one FPGA vendor in place of two. [Fourth-edition contents](SOURCES.md#e4) · [Fifth-edition preface, pp. ix and xi](SOURCES.md#e5-preface)

- **CMOS and switch behavior.** Section 2.9, p. 66, combines basic CMOS with logic gates and includes a short logic-family overview. The semiconductor appendix (p. 507) retains transmission gates and Verilog switch-level models on pp. 514–519. [Fifth-edition body](SOURCES.md#e5-body)
- **Karnaugh-map scope.** The fourth edition has a dedicated five-variable section (§3.4, p. 81). The fifth edition's Ch. 3 acknowledges five-variable maps and teaches maps through four variables. The §8.5 design example still refers to a five-variable case. [Fourth-edition body](SOURCES.md#e4-body) · [Fifth-edition body](SOURCES.md#e5-body)
- **Labs.** The fourth edition includes an asynchronous circuit experiment in §11.19, p. 553, and Verilog simulation and FPGA prototyping in §11.20, p. 553. The fifth goes directly from Experiment 17 (§9.18, p. 478) to Verilog simulation and FPGA prototyping (§9.19, p. 480).
